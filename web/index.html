How MCRC works

Four puzzles stacked on top of each other. Every step is reversible, so decryption is just the steps in reverse.

1

Clockface swap

The point of this stage is the key. It's the ONLY step that uses the time — so this is what makes the same word come out differently when you change the time.

The alphabet is laid out in two rows:

  • Inner row: A B C D E F G H I J K L
  • Outer row: N O P Q R S T U V W X Y
  • M and Z sit in the centre and never move.

The key gives you two slide amounts, one per row — the hour hand says how far inner letters slide, the minute hand (÷ 5) says how far outer letters slide:

  • Key 3:20 → inner slide = 3 (hour), outer slide = 4 (20 ÷ 5).
  • Key 7:45 → inner slide = 7, outer slide = 9.
  • Key 12:00 → both slides are 0. Letters just swap straight across.

To encrypt each letter, do two moves:

  1. Slide the letter sideways along its own row by that row's amount.
  2. Swap straight across to the other row at the new column.

Worked example at 3:20:

  • Q is outer at column 4. Outer slide = 4. 4 + 4 = col 8. Swap to inner col 8 → H. So Q → H.
  • A is inner at column 1. Inner slide = 3. 1 + 3 = col 4. Swap to outer col 4 → Q. So A → Q.

Why two different slides? Because the hour and minute hands point at different numbers, they give independent amounts. That's what makes the key space bigger (12 hours × 12 minute positions = 144 possible keys) and makes the cipher actually depend on the time.

2

Mirror the letters

Reverse the string. That's it. DHRRA becomes ARRHD.

3

Reverse cipher (letters → numbers)

Use an inverted alphabet chart: A = 25, B = 24, …, Z = 00. Each letter becomes a two-digit code joined by dashes.

4

Mirror the numbers

Reverse the list of codes. 25-08-08-18-22 becomes 22-18-08-08-25. Done.
